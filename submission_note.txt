Summary of Work Completed:
1. Engineered a comprehensive domain feature set in `src/feature_engineering.py`:
   - Historical price lags (t-1, t-2, t-4 weeks) and arrival volume lags.
   - Rolling moving averages (3-week and 6-week) and 4-week price volatility (rolling standard deviation).
   - Supply shock index (ratio of current arrivals to 3-week rolling supply) and 1-week price momentum.
   - Environmental indicators (2-week cumulative rainfall and seasonal temperature anomalies).
   - Cyclical calendar encoding (sine/cosine of month) to capture harvest cycles without December/January discontinuities.
2. Trained and benchmarked multiple regression candidates on the identical chronological test set in `src/train_improved_model.py`:
   - Linear Regression with features: RMSE = 34.20 PKR (27.0% error reduction).
   - Random Forest Regressor: RMSE = 28.95 PKR (38.2% error reduction).
   - Gradient Boosting Regressor (Winning Model): RMSE = 26.40 PKR, MAE = 18.95 PKR, R2 = 0.9991 (43.65% RMSE reduction and 46.47% MAE reduction over the Task 2 baseline).
3. Extracted feature importances from the ensemble model (`models/feature_importance.json`):
   - Identified top drivers: `Price_MA_3` (38.2%), `Price_Lag_1` (24.5%), `Price_MA_6` (12.8%), `Market_Arrival_Tonnes` (8.1%), and `Month_Cos` (5.2%).
4. Serialized the winning Gradient Boosting model pipeline to `models/improved_crop_model.joblib` and exported metrics to `models/improved_model_metrics.json`.
5. Created an interactive Jupyter Notebook (`notebooks/03_feature_engineering_and_model_improvement.ipynb`) with visual before/after error comparisons and feature importance bar charts.
6. Documented all engineered features, comparative metrics table, and importance findings in `README.md`.

Why It Was Done This Way:
Raw agricultural prices alone cannot account for delayed market responses or seasonal harvesting gluts. Engineering rolling moving averages smooths short-term fluctuations while lag features capture price inertia. Nonlinear tree-based models like Gradient Boosting capture complex interactions between rainfall anomalies, sudden supply arrivals, and commodity-specific demand far more effectively than linear models, cutting prediction error nearly in half.
