Summary of Work Completed:
1. Formulated the next-week price forecasting objective by shifting current weekly modal prices (t+1) per crop to construct the supervised regression target (Target_Next_Week_Price).
2. Applied an 80/20 chronological train/test split (cutoff: May 2024) across the 2022-2024 time-series to prevent temporal lookahead leakage.
3. Implemented a Naive Persistence Baseline (predicting next week's price equals this week's price) establishing benchmark metrics: RMSE = 56.12 PKR, MAE = 42.13 PKR, MAPE = 1.48%.
4. Built a Scikit-Learn Linear Regression pipeline (src/train_baseline.py) with OneHotEncoding for crops and StandardScaler for market arrival volume, weather indices, and price bounds.
5. Evaluated the model on the unseen test set: RMSE = 46.85 PKR, MAE = 35.40 PKR, R2 = 0.9971, MAPE = 1.22%, achieving a 16.52% reduction in RMSE and 15.97% reduction in MAE over the naive baseline.
6. Serialized the trained pipeline to `models/baseline_linear_regression.joblib` and exported full metrics to `models/baseline_metrics.json`.
7. Created an exploratory Jupyter Notebook (`notebooks/02_baseline_model_training.ipynb`) with visual residual and prediction trajectory comparisons.
8. Documented the baseline methodology, split strategy, and comparative results in `README.md`.

Why It Was Done This Way:
In time-series econometrics, standard random k-fold splitting causes severe data leakage because future information inadvertently leaks into training folds. A chronological split reflects real-world deployment where models only observe past history. Benchmarking against a Naive Persistence Baseline is crucial to confirm that the regression model genuinely captures underlying supply-demand signals rather than trivial price inertia.
